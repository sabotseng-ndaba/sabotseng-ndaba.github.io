EVIDENCE

From one long list to organised work

Our project initially had one long Product Backlog containing tasks required to plan, design and develop the website. Work included defining the business idea, preparing presentations, gathering requirements, designing the interface, organising the GitHub repository, developing website features, testing and deployment.

Following feedback from the lecturer, our team divided the backlog tasks into individual sprints using Trello. The Trello screenshots included in this portfolio should show the tasks from the original backlog organised across the project’s sprints.

Examples of backlog work

  • Define and present the BrightStart business idea.
  • Prepare and present the Figma prototype.
  • Organise Trello boards, add dates and allocate tasks.
  • Set up and maintain the GitHub repository.
  • Develop, test and deploy the website.

PERSONAL REFLECTION

What I learned

I learned that a Product Backlog provides an overall list of the work required for a project, but the tasks also need to be organised in a way that makes them manageable. Our original backlog was one long list. After receiving feedback from the lecturer, we divided the tasks into sprints using Trello.

I contributed by helping organise the tasks, update the board and communicate with team members about outstanding work. One challenge was keeping the board updated while dealing with inconsistent participation and connectivity problems.

This experience taught me to respond to feedback and improve project planning when necessary. In future projects, I would organise the backlog earlier, agree on task priorities with the team and make sure responsibilities are clearly allocated.
