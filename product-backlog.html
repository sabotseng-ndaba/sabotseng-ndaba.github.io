EVIDENCE

From One Long List to Organised Work

Our project initially had one long Product Backlog containing tasks required to plan, design and develop the BrightStart English Tutoring website. The tasks covered business planning, research, requirements, design, development, testing and delivery.

Following feedback from the lecturer, our team reorganised the backlog by dividing the work into individual sprints using Trello. This made it easier to group related tasks, allocate responsibilities and monitor progress throughout the project.

1. Business Idea and Project Planning

These backlog items helped the team establish the purpose of the project and plan the proposed solution.

  • Brainstorm and define the business idea.
  • Identify the problem and target users.
  • Research the community and market.
  • Conduct a feasibility analysis.
  • Prepare and present the business proposal.
  • Review lecturer feedback and improve the proposal.

2. Requirements and Product Planning

These tasks helped the team identify what the solution needed to provide and how the work would be organised.

  • Identify stakeholders.
  • Gather and document system requirements.
  • Define the minimum viable product (MVP).
  • Define the proposed system features.
  • Create and prioritise the Product Backlog.
  • Divide backlog work into sprints.

3. User Interface and Prototype Design

The design activities helped the team plan the appearance and structure of the website before and during development.

  • Collect and analyse survey responses.
  • Create initial wireframes.
  • Design the website interface in Figma.
  • Prepare and present the prototype.
  • Review and improve the design using feedback.

4. Project and Repository Management

These tasks supported collaboration, planning and the organisation of the team's work.

  • Set up and organise the Trello board.
  • Add sprint tasks and relevant dates.
  • Allocate tasks and monitor outstanding work.
  • Set up the GitHub repository.
  • Organise the project structure and code.
  • Coordinate team communication and presentations.

5. Website Development

The development work focused on building the planned BrightStart website and its main pages.

  • Develop the home page.
  • Develop the About Us and Services pages.
  • Develop the registration page and form validation.
  • Develop the quiz page and quiz functionality.
  • Develop the progress page.
  • Develop the contact page.
  • Apply styling and responsive design.
  • Integrate website components.

6. Testing and Final Delivery

The final group of backlog items focused on checking the website, resolving problems and preparing the project for presentation and submission.

  • Test website navigation and forms.
  • Test quiz functionality.
  • Identify and fix website bugs.
  • Review the completed website.
  • Prepare final project documentation.
  • Deploy and test the website.
  • Prepare and deliver the final presentation.
  • Submit the project and review lessons learned.

How the Backlog Was Managed

The Product Backlog represented the overall work required for the project. The team used Trello to organise tasks into sprints, assign responsibilities and track progress. This helped turn a long list of activities into smaller groups of work that were easier to manage.

PERSONAL REFLECTION

What I Learned

I learned that a Product Backlog provides an overall list of the work required for a project, but the tasks also need to be organised in a way that makes them manageable. Our original backlog was one long list. After receiving feedback from the lecturer, we divided the tasks into sprints using Trello.

As Product Owner, I contributed by helping organise tasks, update the board and communicate with team members about outstanding work. One challenge was keeping the board updated while dealing with inconsistent participation and connectivity problems.

This experience taught me to respond to feedback and improve project planning when necessary. In future projects, I would organise the backlog earlier, agree on task priorities with the team and make sure responsibilities are clearly allocated.

I also learned that a backlog needs regular review. Tasks may need to be clarified, reprioritised or divided into smaller activities as the project develops. Keeping the backlog organised helps the team understand what needs attention and what should be done next.
