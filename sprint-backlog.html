Sprint Backlog

How the team organised and monitored project tasks in Trello.

EVIDENCE

Tasks organised into sprints

The BrightStart team used Trello to organise project work across 15 sprints. Tasks covered planning the business idea, preparing presentations, designing the interface, organising the GitHub repository, developing website features, testing and deployment.

After the lecturer instructed us to divide the original Product Backlog into sprints, I helped organise sprint tasks, update the Trello board, add dates and follow up on task allocation with team members.

Evidence to add: Insert Trello screenshots showing sprint boards, dates, tasks and user stories where available. Some sprints did not have user stories recorded; treat this as an improvement area rather than creating evidence that does not exist.
PERSONAL REFLECTION

What I learned

Working with the Sprint Backlog taught me how to break a large project into smaller stages. Instead of working from one long list of tasks, we organised the work into sprints on Trello after receiving feedback from the lecturer.

As Product Owner, I helped organise the sprint tasks, add dates, follow up on task allocation and remind team members about outstanding work. This helped me understand the importance of planning, communication and monitoring progress throughout a project.

One challenge was that some team members were not consistently available, and connectivity and technical difficulties sometimes affected our progress. Some tasks and user stories also needed further organisation.

I learned that creating sprint boards is only one part of Agile planning. The team must also agree on achievable goals, assign responsibilities clearly, update task statuses and review unfinished work. In future projects, I would make sure each sprint has a clear goal, tasks have appropriate owners and progress is reviewed regularly.
