EVIDENCE

Organising the Product Backlog into Sprints

The BrightStart team initially had one long Product Backlog containing tasks for planning, designing and developing the English Tutoring website. After receiving feedback from the lecturer, we reorganised the work into individual sprints using Trello.

The project work covered business planning, requirements gathering, wireframes and prototyping, website development, testing, deployment and final submission. The sprint structure helped us divide this work into smaller stages and monitor the tasks that needed attention.

1. Business Planning and Research

The early project tasks focused on understanding the business idea, identifying the problem and planning the proposed solution.

  • Review the project brief.
  • Form the project team and define team roles.
  • Identify the business idea and target users.
  • Conduct market research and feasibility analysis.
  • Prepare and present the business proposal.

2. Requirements and Product Planning

The team worked on identifying what the proposed solution needed to provide and organising the work required for the project.

  • Identify stakeholders.
  • Gather system requirements.
  • Define the minimum viable product (MVP).
  • Identify and prioritise system features.
  • Create and organise the Product Backlog.
  • Divide backlog tasks into sprints.

3. Wireframes and Prototype Design

Design activities helped the team plan the website's layout and user interface before and during development.

  • Collect and analyse survey responses.
  • Create initial wireframes.
  • Design the website prototype in Figma.
  • Present the prototype and receive feedback.
  • Review and improve the proposed design.

4. Project Organisation and GitHub

These activities supported collaboration, task allocation and the management of project files.

  • Set up and maintain the Trello board.
  • Organise sprint tasks and add relevant dates.
  • Follow up on task allocation and outstanding work.
  • Set up the GitHub repository.
  • Organise the project structure and code.

5. Website Development

The development tasks focused on building the main pages and functionality planned for BrightStart English Tutoring.

  • Develop the home page.
  • Develop the About Us and Services pages.
  • Develop the registration page and form validation.
  • Develop the quiz page and quiz functionality.
  • Develop the progress and contact pages.
  • Apply website styling and responsive design.
  • Integrate website components.

6. Testing and Final Delivery

Later project tasks focused on checking the website, improving its quality and preparing the final deliverables.

  • Test website navigation and registration forms.
  • Test quiz functionality.
  • Identify and fix website bugs.
  • Review the website's responsiveness and interface.
  • Prepare final project documentation.
  • Deploy the website and conduct final testing.
  • Prepare and deliver the final presentation.
  • Submit the project and conduct a retrospective.

How Trello Was Used

Trello was used to organise project tasks, group work into sprints, add dates and monitor outstanding activities. As Product Owner, I helped organise the sprint tasks, update the board and follow up with team members about task allocation and progress.

PERSONAL REFLECTION

What I Learned

Working with the Sprint Backlog taught me how to break a large project into smaller stages. Instead of working from one long list of tasks, we organised the work into sprints on Trello after receiving feedback from the lecturer.

As Product Owner, I helped organise sprint tasks, add dates, follow up on task allocation and remind team members about outstanding work. This helped me understand the importance of planning, communication and monitoring progress throughout a project.

One challenge was that some team members were not consistently available. Connectivity and technical difficulties also sometimes affected our progress. Some tasks and user stories needed further organisation.

I learned that creating sprint boards is only one part of Agile planning. The team must also agree on achievable goals, assign responsibilities clearly, update task statuses and review unfinished work.

In future projects, I would make sure each sprint has a clear goal, tasks have appropriate owners and progress is reviewed regularly. I would also ensure that user stories and task updates are recorded consistently so that the backlog provides a clearer picture of the team's progress.
