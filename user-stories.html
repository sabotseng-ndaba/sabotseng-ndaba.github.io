Our team used Trello to organise project work, divide tasks into sprints and monitor progress. The board covered the project from the initial business idea and research to website development, testing and final submission.

The following user stories have been mapped to the task cards recorded in our Trello board. They explain the purpose of the work completed or planned during each sprint.

User story format: As a [type of user], I want [a feature or action], so that [the benefit].

Note on the evidence

These user stories were mapped to the tasks recorded in the Trello board to make the purpose of each sprint clearer. They are reconstructed descriptions based on the recorded work, not a claim that all 15 stories appeared in the original Trello board.

How User Stories Support Tasks

User stories explain who needs something, what they need and why it is valuable. Related task cards break the work into manageable activities that the team can assign, complete and track.

Connecting stories to tasks makes it easier to understand how individual activities contribute to the overall BrightStart English Tutoring project.

PERSONAL REFLECTION

What I Learned

Organising user stories and tasks helped me understand that project work should focus on what users need and why those needs matter. Tasks then break the work into actions that the team can plan and complete.

As Product Owner, I helped coordinate project work, communicate outstanding tasks and organise the Trello board. One area that needed improvement was making sure stories and tasks were recorded consistently across the sprints.

I learned that user stories should be clear enough for the team to understand the expected outcome. In future projects, I would review stories with the team, include clear acceptance criteria where appropriate, and make sure related tasks are assigned and tracked.

I also learned that a task checklist alone does not always explain the value of the work to the user. Recording a clear user story for each relevant feature would make sprint planning and progress reviews more effective.
